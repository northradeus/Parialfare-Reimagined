# Salarean "Salmon"

**Salmon** is a member of the black operations unit [LENDERMAIRE](/km/factions/lendermaire), recorded in earlier material as "Hard to Kill." He is Lurkish and served in the Lurkish Navy before his recruitment. He survived the sinking of his ship and is the only member of that ship's company recorded as having returned. He tells the old Lurkish naval stories in the household. The record does not establish why.

## 1. Service

Salmon served in the Lurkish coastal guard and then in the Lurkish Navy. The record does not establish the date he enlisted, the vessel on which he served, or the rank he held. He survived the sinking of his ship. The location, circumstances, and cause of the sinking are not recorded. No subsequent service record has been located, and it is not established when or how he left the Navy.

## 2. The ship-brother

Lurkish naval service pairs sailors administratively, two to a slot. The suffix *-mon* marks the pairing. The suffix is permanent: it is not removed on death, transfer, or discharge. Salmon's pairing was with a sailor recorded as Kalmon.

Lurkish naval tradition holds the salmon to be a bad omen for paired sailors.

Kalmon is listed as lost at sea in the sinking. The Navy has closed his record. On at least one recorded occasion, Salmon named Kalmon and stated that he does not accept the Navy's finding. The record does not describe the basis of his position. Salmon is listed in the Navy's record as *unmatched*, meaning his pairing slot remains open. The Navy offered reassignment. The record documents the offer and does not document a response.

Salmon asks persons arriving from Lurkish ports and coasts whether they know of Kalmon or of the sinking. The record does not state what answers he has received. He reads published lists of recovered sailors and released prisoners when they reach the household. The record does not establish how they reach it. No list recorded to date names Kalmon.

## 3. The unmatched

The Lurkish Navy does not hold a ritual for the loss of a ship-brother. It records the loss, offers reassignment, and continues. The word for a sailor whose pairing is broken is *unmatched*. Salmon remains unmatched.

*Unmatched* is an administrative state, not a formal observance. The Navy does not require a sailor to accept or reject it. Salmon has neither accepted nor rejected it.

The record does not establish when the *-mon* suffix was adopted, or when Salmon replaced Salarean in common use.

## 4. The old stories

Salmon tells the old Lurkish naval stories in the household. The stories take four recorded forms:

- **The Last Voyage.** The log of a ship that did not return, told in log format. The log ends mid-sentence.
- **The Cold Watch.** A sailor alone on watch who does not report what he sees, told as a report. The report is missing its final entry.
- **The Reading of Names.** The names of the lost, read aloud at port before leave, in order, once.
- **The Count.** The tally a Lurkish sailor keeps of voyages and losses.

The household knows that Salmon is Lurkish and that the stories are Lurkish naval customs. It treats them as one of Salmon's habits, alongside the unresolved role split between [Grunt](/km/characters/lendermaire/grunt) and [Gunter](/km/characters/lendermaire/gunter). No member of the household has asked him why he tells them. [Lykor](/km/characters/samsara-lykor), the only other Lurkish member of the unit, hears the stories. He does not say what he hears.

The record does not describe what Salmon recites. It notes only that he recites.

## 5. In the household

Salmon was recruited to LENDERMAIRE by [Konish Kimbotin](/characters/lendermaire/konish-kimbotin-(reimagined)). The record does not establish how the recruitment took place. No member of the household has stated that they know why Kimbotin chose him.

Salmon's physical resilience is recorded and is the origin of his earlier epithet. He is recorded as performing well underwater. The record does not explain either.

Salmon is listed in the household as a shock trooper. When the stories are not being told, he is recorded on the household's night watch. The record does not state whether any member has discussed the stories outside Salmon's presence. It does not state whether Kimbotin has been present for a telling.

## 6. Death

Salmon is killed in action during a post-2008 engagement by an airstrike flown by the [Anajak Forces](/km/factions/anajak-forces) strike pilot [Takahashi](/km/characters/anajak-forces/takahashi) (codename **"Sahara"**). He is the first confirmed fatality among LENDERMAIRE's thirteen core members.

The record notes the coincidence of the two names without attributing intent to either side: the sailor who took the name of the fish held by Lurkish naval tradition to be a bad omen, and who was recorded in early material as "Hard to Kill," was killed from the air by a pilot operating under the Lurkish word for a giant sea bird.

At the time of his death, his administrative pairing in the Lurkish Navy remains *unmatched*, and his inquiries into the fate of his ship-brother Kalmon end without a recorded answer. Within the household, the four Lurkish naval stories cease with his death, leaving [Lykor](/km/characters/samsara-lykor) as the only remaining Lurkish member under the roof.

## Unresolved

- **The airstrike:** the date, location, and operation in which Takahashi's strike killed him.
- **The sinking:** the location, circumstances, and cause of the sinking that left Salmon the sole survivor of his ship.
- **Kalmon's status:** whether the ship-brother is alive. Salmon stated, on at least one recorded occasion, that he did not accept the Navy's finding. What happened to Kalmon beyond his loss at sea is not recorded.
- **What Salmon witnessed:** whether he was on watch at the time of the sinking, and what he saw. Not recorded.
- **The reassignment:** whether Salmon declined the offer formally or never responded.
- **The name:** when he adopted the *-mon* suffix, and when Salmon replaced Salarean in the record.
- **The recruitment:** how Kimbotin found Salmon, and why he chose him.
- **The inquiries:** what answers Salmon received before his death, and how the published lists reached the household.
- **The audience:** whether Kimbotin was ever present for a telling of the stories.
